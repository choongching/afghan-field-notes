# Afghanistan: physical geography for a traveller's sketch map

Research notes to support a hand-drawn "traveller's sketch map" used as the backdrop of a travel photo diary.
Coordinates are given as **lon, lat** in decimal degrees (x, y order for drawing).
Coordinates marked **~** are approximate: they are my own reading of the map or an interpolation, not a published point.
Coordinates without ~ come from a cited source, which is usually a Wikipedia infobox cross-checked against the narrative sources.

## 0. Frame

- Country bounding box: about lon 60.5–74.9, lat 29.4–38.5. The country's centre is about 65.0, 33.0.
- Iranica: the country runs from the Iranian border at 61°31′E to the Wakhan "handle" in the east, and covers about 650,000 km².
- LoC country study: more than 49% of the land lies above 2,000 m.
- Lowest point: 258 m, on the Amu Darya at Khamyab (~65.7, 37.5).
- Highest point: Noshaq, 7,492 m. The LoC country study gives 7,485 m.

## 1. Physiographic regions

Britannica and the LoC study use three macro-regions. Iranica subdivides the country into 11 zones. The sub-regions below are listed from northeast to southwest.

| Region | Approx. extent (lon, lat) | Typical elevation | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wakhan / Pamir** | ~71.5–74.9, 36.5–37.6. Walls of permanent snow and glaciers stand on both sides. |
| **Badakhshan** | ~69.5–71.8, 35.8–38.3 | Mostly 4,000–5,000 m; peaks above 6,000 m | Deeply cut, steep ranges with glaciers and alpine lakes. The Kokcha valley and Faizabad lie here. |

## 2. Mountain ranges: ridge-axis polylines (lon, lat)

All of these polylines are **approximate**. I traced them from the described extents and the known passes and peaks along each ridge, so treat them as sketch guides rather than survey lines.

- **Hindu Kush, main axis NE → SW.** Wikipedia gives about 800 km in total; the LoC study gives 966 km long and about 240 km across.
  `(73.40,36.85) → (72.40,36.60) → (71.83,36.43) Noshaq → (71.15,36.10) Dorah Pass area → (70.40,35.95) → (70.01,36.02) Anjuman Pass → (69.04,35.31) Salang → (68.26,34.91) Shibar`
  A secondary spur runs from Koh-e Bandaka at 70.98, 36.18 northwest into Badakhshan.
- **Koh-i-Baba.** This is the western continuation of the Hindu Kush. It is the source of the Helmand, Kabul, Kunduz and Hari Rud, and its crest is about 5,000 m.
  `(68.30,34.75) → (67.90,34.68) → (67.62,34.65) Shah Fuladi → (67.10,34.58) → (66.60,34.55) → (66.00,34.50)`
- **Safed Koh / Paropamisus ("White Mountains") of Herat.** The range runs about 480 km along the north bank of the Hari Rud. Its high point is 3,592 m at 63.03, 34.47.
  `(65.60,34.70) → (64.80,34.60) → (64.00,34.55) → (63.03,34.47) → (62.40,34.60) → (61.60,34.80)`
- **Siah Koh ("Black Mountains").** The range lies south of the Hari Rud and runs parallel to the Safed Koh. It is roughly 3,000–3,500 m high.
  `(65.20,34.20) → (64.40,34.15) → (63.60,34.10) → (62.90,34.10)`
- **Band-e Turkestan.** The range lies north of the Murghab headwaters and faces the northern plains. It is roughly 3,000–3,500 m high.
  `(66.20,35.60) → (65.50,35.55) → (64.80,35.50) → (64.10,35.40) → (63.60,35.35)`
- **Spin Ghar / Safed Koh (east).** The range runs about 160 km west–east from the Logar valley to the Peshawar valley. Its high point is Sikaram, 4,755 m, at 70.37, 33.97. Tora Bora lies on its north face.
  `(69.30,33.95) → (69.80,34.00) → (70.37,33.97) Sikaram → (70.80,34.05) → (71.10,34.10) Khyber`
- **Sulaiman edge.** The Sulaiman proper lies in Pakistan; on the Afghan side are its foothills and the border ridges of Paktika, Zabul and Kandahar.
  `(70.30,33.30) → (69.90,32.80) → (69.40,32.00) → (68.60,31.70) → (67.60,31.40) → (66.70,31.10) → (66.40,30.40)`
- **Pamir / Wakhan ranges.**
  - The Hindu Kush wall south of the Wakhan follows the Pakistan border, with many peaks around 6,000–7,000 m: `(71.60,36.50) → (72.30,36.75) → (73.00,36.85) → (73.70,36.90) → (74.50,37.00)`.
  - The Nicholas (Big Pamir) range separates the Great and Little Pamirs: `(72.80,37.30) → (73.40,37.30) → (74.00,37.20) → (74.40,37.15)`.
  - The Great Pamir crest runs north of that and borders Tajikistan along the Pamir River and Zorkul, which is at about 73.7, 37.45.

## 4. Deserts and basins (extents approximate)

- **Registan.** Iranica classes it as a sandy desert. It covers ~64.0–66.6, 29.9–31.5 and has a reference point at 65.41, 30.45. It consists of red sand hills 15–30 m high, formed as transverse dunes by west-to-east winds, with sand plains and rocky or clay patches between them. It is spreading west onto farmland.
- **Dasht-e Margo ("Desert of Death").** It covers ~62.3–64.2, 29.9–31.4 and is centred about 63.0, 31.0. Wikipedia gives about 150,000 km², though that likely includes the neighbouring deserts. It lies at 500–700 m. The surface is rocky and clayey plain with salt flats (solonchak and takyr) and desert pavement.
- **Dasht-e Khash.** It covers ~62.3–63.8, 31.4–32.3, north of the Dasht-e Margo and between the Farah and Khash rivers. It is stony desert pavement at about 700 m.
- **Sistan basin and the Hamun lakes.** The whole system is centred about 61.67, 30.83. Water circulates from Hamun-e Puzak in the northeast (~61.8, 31.5) into Hamun-e Saberi (~61.3, 31.3, on the border) and then overflows into Hamun-e Helmand to the southwest (~61.2, 30.9, in Iran). In high-water years, overflow runs south along the Shela channel to the **Gowd-e Zereh** salt pan (~61.8, 29.8). The Hamuns are fed by the Helmand, Farah, Khash and Harut rivers. They are reed marsh in wet years and white salt crust in dry ones.
- **Dasht-e Nawur** (~67.8, 33.7) is a high endorheic basin in Ghazni at about 3,100 m. **Ab-e Istada** (~67.9, 32.5) is a saline lake between Ghazni and Kandahar.
- **Northern sand belt**: dunes and takyr along the Amu Darya west of Mazar, at ~64.5–67.0, 36.9–37.4.

## 5. Rivers: sources, courses and endpoints (lon, lat)

The LoC study gives these lengths: Amu Darya 1,100 km within Afghanistan, Helmand about 1,300 km, Hari Rud 650 km within Afghanistan, Kabul 460 km. Iranica says the Helmand drains about 40% of the country, and that only the Kabul reaches the sea (through the Indus).

- **Panj / Amu Darya.**
  - Sources: the Wakhan River, from the Wakhjir Pass (74.48, 37.09) through Chaqmaqtin Kol (~74.3, 37.1), and the Pamir River, from Zorkul (~73.7, 37.45).
  - They meet at Qala-e Panja (~72.6, 37.0) to form the Panj.
  - The Panj runs west to Ishkashim (~71.6, 36.7), then turns north past Khorog (~71.55, 37.5), loops round the Darvaz bulge (~71.0, 38.4 at its northernmost), and runs SW down to the Kokcha mouth (69.40, 37.16).
  - It is joined by the Vakhsh at ~68.3, 37.1, and from there it is called the Amu Darya.
  - It continues past Termez (~67.3, 37.2) and Kelif (~66.3, 37.35) and leaves the border at Khamyab (~65.7, 37.5).
  - It ends in the Aral Sea delta (~59.5, 43.7).
- **Kabul River.** It rises at 68.84, 34.36 (Sanglakh range, near Maidan Shar) and flows east past Kabul (69.17, 34.53), through the Tang-e Gharu gorge (~69.4, 34.55), to Surobi and the Naghlu reservoir (~69.7, 34.6). It passes Jalalabad (70.45, 34.43), where the Kunar joins from the NE, cuts north of the Khyber (~71.1, 34.2), and continues past Peshawar to the Indus at Attock (72.23, 33.92). It is about 700 km long in total. Tributaries: the Logar (from the S), the Panjshir and Ghorband (from the N), and the Kunar.
- **Helmand.** It rises at 68.55, 34.57, about 40 km W of Kabul, and flows SW through Daykundi and Uruzgan. Along the way:
  - the Kajaki Dam (~65.12, 32.32);
  - Gereshk (~64.57, 31.82) and Lashkar Gah (~64.37, 31.59);
  - the Arghandab confluence (64.38, 31.45);
  - south through the desert to a big bend at Chahar Burjak (~62.1, 30.3), where it turns N;
  - the Sistan marshes (61.55, 31.15).

  Wikipedia gives 1,150 km, Iranica and the LoC study about 1,300 km.
- **Arghandab.** It rises in the Ghazni and Nawur uplands (~67.5, 33.3) and flows SW past the Dahla Dam (~65.9, 31.85) and Kandahar (65.7, 31.6) to the Helmand at 64.38, 31.45. It is about 560 km long (Iranica).
- **Hari Rud.** It rises at 66.72, 34.65 in the Koh-i-Baba and flows W past Chaghcharan (~65.25, 34.52), the Minaret of Jam (64.52, 34.40), Obe (~63.17, 34.37), and south of Herat (62.2, 34.33). Near Islam Qala and Kohsan (~61.2, 34.5) it turns N and forms the Iran border past Zulfiqar (~61.1, 35.6) and Sarakhs (~61.15, 36.5). It dies in the Karakum beyond Tejen (~60.4, 37.4). The total length is about 1,100 km, of which 650 km is in Afghanistan.
- **Kunduz (Surkhab).** It rises on the north side of the Koh-i-Baba, about 20 km SW of Bamiyan (~67.7, 34.7), and flows N through Doshi (~68.67, 35.62), Pul-i-Khumri (~68.71, 35.95), Baghlan (~68.7, 36.13) and Kunduz (68.86, 36.73). It is joined by the Khanabad River and reaches the Panj / Amu Darya at ~68.4, 37.05. Its basin is about 31,300 km².
- **Kokcha.** It rises at 70.72, 36.03 (Kuran wa Munjan) and flows N past Jurm and Baharak and W past Faizabad (~70.58, 37.12). It enters the Panj at 69.40, 37.16 near Dasht-e Qala. It is 320 km long.
- **Balkh (Band-e Amir) River.** It rises at the Band-e Amir lakes (67.23, 34.84), flows W and then N through gorges (~66.9, 36.3 at its exit, marked on the map at 66.93, 36.65), and fans into irrigation canals around Balkh and Mazar (~66.9, 36.75). It no longer reaches the Amu Darya.
- **Murghab.** It rises at 65.98, 35.15 in the Paropamisus of Ghor and flows W between the Safed Koh and the Band-e Turkestan to Bala Murghab (~63.33, 35.58), then N past Maruchak (~63.1, 35.8). It forms the Turkmen border for about 16 km and ends in the Merv / Mary oasis (~61.8, 37.6). It is about 850 km long.
- **Farah Rud.** It rises in the Band-e Bayan in E Ghor (~65.0, 33.8) and flows SW past Farah (62.11, 32.37) to Hamun-e Saberi / Puzak (~61.6, 31.5). It is about 560 km long.
- Others: the Khash Rud (~63.5, 32.5 → Hamun-e Puzak) and the Harut / Adraskan (~62.4, 33.5 → Hamun-e Saberi).

## 7. How to draw it (traveller's sketch map)

The spine of the drawing is the **Hindu Kush → Koh-i-Baba → Safed Koh arc**, running from the top right to the centre-left. Put the most ink here. Everything else hangs off it.

1. **Hindu Kush and the Wakhan (heaviest weight).** Draw tall jagged peaks with snow caps: leave the tops white and put heavy cross-hatching on the shaded sides. Draw them in a dense row along the NE–SW polyline, largest near Noshaq and Bandaka. Add a few glacier tongues and a thin river thread down the Wakhan with small "pamir" grass tufts. Mark the Salang with a tiny zigzag road and tunnel-mouth arch. **Weight: 30%.**
2. **Hazarajat / Koh-i-Baba (strong).** Draw rounded, bare, high ridges with lighter, broken hatching and snow on the crest only. Show Bamiyan as a green strip with a cliff and two small niche arches, and Band-e Amir as blue droplets on a pale plateau. Add terraced field lines in the valleys (short parallel curved strokes). **Weight: 20%.**
3. **Western ridges (Safed Koh, Siah Koh, Band-e Turkestan) (medium).** Draw three long, low parallel ridge lines with sparse hatching. Show the Hari Rud as a ribbon between them, with the Minaret of Jam as a tiny vertical tower in a gorge and Herat as a walled square with a citadel. **Weight: 10%.**
4. **Northern plains (light but textured).** Draw smooth, rolling loess hills as soft overlapping arcs, with no hatching or only a light stipple. Show oases as small tree clusters, the Blue Mosque at Mazar as a dome with a light colour wash, and the Amu Darya as a broad braided double line along the whole northern edge. **Weight: 12%.**
5. **Eastern basins (Kabul, Panjshir, Jalalabad) (medium, detailed).** Draw Kabul as a bowl of stippled flat ground with ridges round it and a small fort (Bala Hissar) and garden terrace (Babur). Show the Panjshir as a steep V-valley with a bright river. Put palms at Jalalabad. Show the Khyber as a winding road through dark hatched hills at the east edge. **Weight: 10%.**
6. **Southwestern plateau and deserts (open, airy).** Keep this area mostly empty paper. Use light, sparse stipple for the stony desert (Dasht-e Margo, Dasht-e Khash) and crescent dune marks, drawn in rows with the horns pointing east, for the Registan in red-ochre. The Helmand and Arghandab are thin green-edged ribbons, and Kandahar is a small oasis. This empty space is the natural place for photos and labels. **Weight: 13%** (mostly negative space).
7. **Sistan / Hamun (accent).** Draw a loose ring of reed marks and wavy water lines at the far west edge, with a pale salt-pan oval for the Gowd-e Zereh to the south. **Weight: 5%.**

Colour guide, if you use wash:

- Snow: white paper.
- High mountains: cool grey-blue shadow.
- Hazarajat: warm grey-ochre.
- Plains: pale sage and wheat.
- Desert: pale sand, with red-ochre in the Registan.
- Rivers and lakes: turquoise (the Band-e Amir is the brightest).

Rivers mostly run **outward from the central massif**: north to the Amu Darya, east to the Indus, west to the Hari Rud and Murghab, and southwest to Sistan. Drawing that radial pattern makes the map read as believable.
